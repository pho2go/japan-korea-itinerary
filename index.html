Traveler tips — Japan

  • IC card: the regular green Suica taps onto trains, subways, buses and convenience-store purchases across Japan (Shinkansen and limited-express tickets are separate; some rural buses are cash-only). Top up with cash at any station machine. Before flying out, hand it in at a JR East ticket office (Narita Airport station) for the ¥500 deposit plus remaining balance, less a ¥220 fee — run the balance down first.
  • Cash: temples, morning markets and small soba shops (Togakushi, Takayama) are often cash-only. 7-Eleven ATMs take US cards and are everywhere.
  • Toshogu (Nov 13): it is across the road from the ryokan — be there at the 9 AM opening, before the tour buses; last entry is mid-afternoon. Nikko souvenirs worth a look: yuba (tofu skin), yokan, Nikko-bori woodcarving.
  • Takayama morning markets (Miyagawa riverside and Jinya-mae) run about 7 AM–noon — a good fit for the Nov 19 rest day.
  • Onsen etiquette: rinse off seated before soaking, no swimsuits, and the small towel stays out of the water. If anyone has tattoos, ask the hotel about private baths.
  • Trash cans are rare — carry a small bag, and eat street food where you bought it rather than walking.
  • Long train rides: buy ekiben (station bento) and drinks before boarding the Shinano and Hida — don't count on a food cart.
  • No tipping in Japan or Korea.

Traveler tips — Korea

  • Maps: Google Maps can't do walking or driving directions in Korea — download Naver Map (English UI) before arrival, plus Papago for translation.
  • T-money card from any convenience store covers subways, buses and most taxis in both Busan and Seoul; top up with cash.
  • Taxis are inexpensive, especially in Busan — use the Kakao T app to hail and skip the language barrier.
  • Seoul palaces: Gyeongbokgung is closed Tuesdays and Changdeokgung is closed Mondays — plan palace visits for the Thu–Sun in Seoul (Nov 30, departure day, is a Monday). Royal guard changing ceremony at Gyeongbokgung: 10 AM and 2 PM.
  • Hanbok rental near the palaces gets everyone free palace entry and photos.
  • Changdeokgung Secret Garden is a separate timed, guided ticket — online release 6 days ahead at 10 AM KST, and it sells out.

If something goes wrong

  • Japan emergency: 110 police, 119 ambulance/fire. US Embassy Tokyo +81 3-3224-5000. Japan Visitor Hotline (English, 24h): 050-3816-2787.
  • Korea emergency: 112 police, 119 ambulance/fire. Korea Travel Hotline 1330 (English, 24h, also interprets). US Embassy Seoul +82 2-397-4114.
  • Missed a train: Japanese reserved tickets can be swapped once for free for a later train the same day at any JR ticket office — don't just board the next one. Non-reserved cars exist on the Hida, Shinano and most Shinkansen (not Kagayaki/Hayabusa).
  • Lost item: Japan's lost-and-found works well — report at the nearest station office or kōban (police box) with the exact train/time; hotels can call for you.
